Heptabase: differentiating against AI itself

Instead of competing on AI features, Heptabase positions against what AI makes too easy, and uses art direction to sell slow, deep thinking.

The job of the page

  • 01Explain why a visual knowledge base matters when AI can just answer
  • 02Make a dense, powerful UI feel calm and approachable
  • 03Show it works with the sources people already read

What works

01Contrarian, two-beat headline

“AI gives you answers. Heptabase builds your worldview.” concedes what AI does well, then claims the space AI can't own: understanding.

02Painterly art direction

The product window floats over a sunset landscape. The softness balances a dense UI and says “thinking”, not “productivity”.

03Segmentation before the headline

An App / Plugin toggle above the H1 lets two audiences self-select before they read a word.

04Sources made concrete

Chips for PDFs, web pages, YouTube, podcasts, Readwise and Zotero turn “import anything” into a list a reader recognises. Awards and investors (YC, Kleiner Perkins) add proof.

Friction & opportunities

!Dense UI at thumbnail size

Screenshots of whiteboards and tables are hard to read at hero scale; the detail that sells the product is too small to see.

!Toggle with no explanation

“App” vs “Plugin” is clear to existing users but ambiguous to a newcomer.

What I'd test next

ExperimentVariantSuccess metric
Annotated hotspotsZoomable hotspots on the hero screenshot explaining 3 key actionsTrial starts; hotspot interaction rate
Explain the toggleAdd a one-line hint (“Use it standalone or inside Claude”) under the toggleToggle usage; plugin installs

Takeaway for my own work

You can differentiate against the trend everyone rides. Art direction can make a complex tool feel calm.

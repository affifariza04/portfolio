Tally: personality in a commodity category

Form builders all promise the same thing. Tally competes on feel: doodles, a doc-like editor and a pricing promise in the hero.

The job of the page

  • 01Stand out against Google Forms and Typeform at first glance
  • 02Make building a form feel as easy as typing a document
  • 03Remove every reason not to try it now

What works

01Illustration as differentiation

Hand-drawn faces, speech bubbles and a scribbled underline under “simplest” give a commodity product a voice. The line art is light enough not to fight the CTA.

02The preview is the mental model

The hero shows the editor itself: a “Customer feedback” title, a slash prompt and a submit button. It reads like a document, which is the whole idea.

03Friction removed in microcopy

“No signup required” under “Create a free form” answers the main objection exactly where the decision happens.

04Pricing as a feature

“Unlimited forms and submissions for free” gets its own highlighted card early, turning the business model into a selling point. Privacy (hosted in Europe, GDPR) follows for teams that need it.

Friction & opportunities

!Long, even rhythm

Many feature cards share the same weight, so after the hero the page loses a clear story arc and scanning visitors may miss the strongest points.

!Logos without numbers

“Powering 2,500,000+ teams” is strong; the logo strip under it is small and grey, so recognisable brands like Notion get little attention.

What I'd test next

ExperimentVariantSuccess metric
Try it in the heroLet visitors type a question into the hero editor vs the static previewForms created in the first session

Takeaway for my own work

In a crowded category, the feel of a product is a feature. Put the objection-killer right under the button.

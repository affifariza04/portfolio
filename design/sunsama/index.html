Sunsama: selling a feeling, not a feature list

Sunsama positions a task planner around calm instead of productivity. The page is built as a before-and-after story.

The job of the page

  • 01Make an overwhelmed professional feel understood
  • 02Explain a daily ritual (plan, focus, review) without a manual
  • 03Lower the risk of trying a paid tool

What works

01A headline that is the product

“Start Calm. Stay Focused. End Confident.” maps one-to-one onto the daily ritual: plan in the morning, focus during the day, review at night.

02Problem, then solution, side by side

A card titled “Work is f***ing chaotic” lists pains with red marks; the next card answers each with a check and a testimonial. The contrast does the persuading.

03A voice people remember

The censored swear word is a deliberate brand risk. It signals a human company, and it filters for the audience that will love the product.

04Risk reversal next to the CTA

“14-day free trial, no credit card” and “Works with Claude, ChatGPT and more” sit beside the orange button, answering cost and compatibility at once.

Friction & opportunities

!Warm gradient vs product clarity

The peach gradient sets the mood but the product view is small in the first screen, so visitors feel the promise before they see the tool.

!Social proof is quiet

Press logos are muted and small; one strong numeric proof point would carry more weight next to the headline.

What I'd test next

ExperimentVariantSuccess metric
Show the daily ritualA 3-step animated strip (plan · focus · review) under the headline vs static copyTrial starts; time on page

Takeaway for my own work

When everyone sells features, sell the state your user wants to be in, then prove it with a before-and-after.

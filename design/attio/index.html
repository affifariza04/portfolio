Attio: selling an AI-native CRM in one line

How Attio's homepage reframes a crowded category (CRM) as a new one (agentic revenue), and where that bet costs clarity.

The job of the page

  • 01Tell a sales leader in five seconds that this is a CRM, but a new kind
  • 02Make AI feel like part of the daily workflow, not a bolt-on
  • 03Offer a self-serve path and a sales path without either competing

What works

01Product as the hero image

The visual is the real app, a deal page with an AI thread asking how to win the GreenLeaf deal, rather than an illustration. It shows the promise instead of describing it.

02A clear two-CTA hierarchy

“Start for free” is the only filled button; “Talk to sales” sits next to it in outline. Both buyer types get a path and the product-led one wins visually.

03Freshness signals above the fold

An announcement bar and a small pill (“GTM lessons from…”) tell returning visitors something is new without moving the headline.

04Restraint as a trust signal

A near-monochrome palette, generous whitespace and one soft lavender gradient read as enterprise-grade and leave all the colour to the product UI.

attio.com · further down

Friction & opportunities

!Category language before category

“Welcome to agentic revenue” is memorable, but a first-time visitor has to read the subcopy to learn it is a CRM. New categories need an anchor in the old one.

!The proof sits below the fold

At a 1440 × 900 viewport the product window starts below the fold, so the first screen is all words and the strongest asset waits for a scroll.

What I'd test next

ExperimentVariantSuccess metric
Anchor the category in the H1“The AI-native CRM for agentic revenue” vs the current headlineStart-for-free click-through; scroll depth to the product frame

Takeaway for my own work

When you name a new category, keep one foot in the old one. Show the product in the first screen; it argues better than copy.
